    Higher depth and MultiPV give more reliable classifications but take longer per move. Good is awarded purely on scarcity now: 1-2 candidates clearly standing out above the mean, by the gap built into that calculation. The "min. spread" threshold handles the opposite extreme — when every candidate is within a hair of every other one (a dead-lost position where everything reads ~0.1%, say), there's no real difference to reward, so the move is Correct outright rather than a meaningless Solid. A Brilliant is a chain: the first Good move, followed by that many of the player's own subsequent moves all landing Good or Solid, with the chain length scaling smoothly between the two values above based on game phase (whichever of material-left or move-count says it's later). The chain is voided only if the opponent played a Mistake or Blunder anywhere in that span — a plain Correct from them doesn't disqualify it, since in a forced sequence that may have been their only reasonable option.
